# The Diverse Landscape of Taiwanese Music
Part of what inspired me to create this blog was because I found myself so fascinated by the incredible diversity that exists within Taiwanese music. For example, you can listen to a French-inspired album by Siri Lee ([李竺芯《水 Súi》](https://youtu.be/ehl-pc0syCU)) and then proceed to listen to Taiwanese Enka by Chiam Nga-Bun ([詹雅雯《貪心》](https://youtu.be/jTIldq-m2cY)). Or listen to the timeless melodies and stories in the songs _Káng-Piⁿ Sioh-Pia̍t_ (吳成家 + 陳達儒《港邊惜別》; 1938) and _An-Pêng Tui-Sióng Khek_ (許石心《安平追想曲》; 1951), then proceed to listen to new compositions from CJ MiT and Bell Chung such as *Chhun-Thiⁿ ê Hoe* ([CJ MiT《春天的花》](https://youtu.be/kiyYBUOZCt)) and _Phàng-Kìⁿ ê Lí_ ([鍾綺 Bell Chung 《紡見的你》](https://youtu.be/_amrF6QwbCU)).

I believe that artists are quite aware of this diversity and evolution, too! In the previously mentioned song, _Phàng-Kìⁿ ê Lí_, the song has a reference to classic songs such as _Ú Iā Hoe_ (鄧雨賢《雨夜花》; 1934) and _Bāng Chhun Hong_ (鄧雨賢《望春風》; 1933), songs that are almost 90 years older. (I believe _Phàng-Kìⁿ ê Lí_ deserves a whole blog post by itself because of how beautiful this song is in terms of the composition and lyrics.) In the song _Bú-Tâi_ ([蕭煌奇《舞台》](https://youtu.be/A-OziFCxXsA); 2021), the song features Ricky Hsiao (蕭煌奇) singing pop, N̂g Péng-Siông (黃昺翔) rapping, and Phoaⁿ Lē-Lē (潘麗麗) singing opera. In this song, the lyrics show that they are all united by their love for performing music (regardless of genre). Similarly, Ricky Hsiao has another song, Tâi-Oân Bī (台灣味) which talks about Taiwanese music throughout the years, making references to Koa-Á-Hì (歌仔戲; Taiwanese Opera) and Jody Chiang (Kang Hūi 江蕙; an iconic Taiwanese singer/quasi-national music icon).
